# Product Vision

- Status: Approved baseline
- Owner: Mad Mallard Solutions
- Last updated: 2026-07-12

## Vision statement

Mad Mallard Platform gives small businesses a practical, affordable operating system for their public web presence, customer intake, messaging, service delivery, and business administration without requiring a collection of expensive disconnected SaaS subscriptions.

## Problem

Small businesses frequently assemble their operations from unrelated tools for websites, link pages, contact forms, messaging, CRM, booking, client portals, affiliate links, analytics, and billing. The result is duplicated data, inconsistent branding, recurring subscription cost, weak ownership of customer information, and operational complexity.

## Product opportunity

Build one modular platform that:

- begins with a useful single-business MVP;
- remains inexpensive to operate;
- supports multiple visually distinct businesses;
- centralizes customer and operational data;
- can add modules gradually without forcing every tenant to use every feature;
- remains self-hostable while preserving a path to a commercial SaaS offering.

## Initial operating context

Mad Mallard Solutions is the product creator and first operating business. Mad Mallard Personal Training and Mad Mallards Adventures are future internal tenants that exercise different modules and branding needs.

## Target outcomes

1. A visitor can discover a business, submit a request, and continue a private conversation.
2. A customer can register, authenticate, and manage their activity.
3. Business staff can manage requests and conversations without exposing platform administration.
4. One deployment can eventually host multiple independent businesses.
5. Infrastructure cost remains justified by real usage.
6. Product and engineering decisions remain traceable in the repository.

## Non-goals for the current MVP

- replacing a full enterprise CRM;
- implementing every planned business module;
- supporting arbitrary third-party plugins;
- production-scale multi-region availability;
- rebuilding the stable admin dashboard without an approved requirement;
- premature microservices.
